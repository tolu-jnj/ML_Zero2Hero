# Problem Framing

## Course Grounding

- The order-monitoring case begins with supply disruption and customer impact, then defines anomaly detection as a way to flag unusual orders. [COURSE: 03 Expert Guide C2 W3 Live Event.pdf#slide 5]
- The Available-to-Promise case responds to inaccurate or unavailable delivery dates and states a goal of more reliable shipping and delivery dates. [COURSE: 04 Expert Guide C2 W4 Live Event.pdf#slide 4]
- Learners are reminded to understand the business process and collaborate with domain experts before choosing a model. [COURSE: 01_Week_1_Transcript.txt#lines 40-60]

## Apply It

1. Name the user, decision, and point in the workflow where a prediction could change an action. [EXTERNAL: established ML project-framing and product-discovery references]
2. Record the current workflow and baseline outcome before proposing ML. [EXTERNAL: established ML project-framing references]
3. Define a primary success metric, a minimum useful threshold, and unacceptable error costs with stakeholders. The course case aligns a model target with business expectations but does not prescribe a reusable threshold-setting method. [COURSE: 04_Week_4_Transcript.txt#lines 190-228] [GAP]
4. Check whether labels, representative data, permissions, and a feasible response action exist. [EXTERNAL: responsible-AI risk-management references]
5. Decide whether deterministic logic or a non-ML process is a better baseline. [EXTERNAL: established ML project-framing references]

## Checklist

- [ ] Error tradeoffs and impact documented.
- [ ] Data access, labels, and operating constraints checked.
